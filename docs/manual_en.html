wDSP Manual

Everything inside the app, the right order to tune it, what it measures and why. Written for the driver, not the programmer.

About the app

In QF head units, audio output from Android passes through a dedicated ROHM BU32107 sound processor. wDSP controls it via the head unit's MCU: the equalizer, door speaker filters, subwoofer, delay channels, and fader. The factory DSP app controls the same chip, but offers far less control.

It is worth understanding early on what the processor can and cannot do:

  • one shared equalizer for all speakers: 16 bands from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, 2 dB steps, ±12 dB limits, with fixed Q factor;
  • no independent EQ per left or right speaker;
  • the subwoofer has dedicated crossover filtering, gain, and delay; door speakers have high-pass filtering and bass boost.

Because of this, cabin acoustic measurement focuses primarily on detecting faults: swapped wires, out-of-phase speakers, unbalanced channels, and door resonances. The equalizer applies broad, sensible corrections. On this hardware platform, textbook flat response down to a decibel does not exist, and the app will not promise it.

First run

On initial launch, the app opens the Permissions Wizard. Enable each permission using the Grant button next to it:

  • Microphone — cabin acoustic measurement and real-time spectrum in Microphone mode;
  • Display over other apps — status bar visualizer widget and full-screen screensaver;
  • Notification access — media track title and album art retrieval;
  • Location / GPS — GALA speed-dependent volume adjustment;
  • Background work — prevents Android from terminating the persistent service managing the DSP processor.

Root access (Magisk) is optional. When available, it allows the app to cleanly acquire exclusive microphone access from voice assistants prior to sweep runs.

Important. Permissions should always be granted directly within the app UI. Do not grant permissions artificially via adb commands, so permission flow integrity remains verified.

Setup sequence

Sequence matters: each step builds upon findings from the previous. Perform all microphone-assisted steps inside the car: doors closed, engine off, silent cabin.

  1. Describe your car

    wDSP Settings → Cabin Acoustic Calibration & Auto-Presets. Here you answer setup parameters that dictate how sweeps are interpreted:

    • The cabin: Closed (a car) or Open (bench, cabriolet). A closed vehicle cabin naturally amplifies deep bass (cabin gain), which the synthesis model takes into account;
    • cabin length, width, and height;
    • Where the microphone is and What surrounds the capsule: affects high-frequency acoustic calibration;
    • whether a subwoofer is present and Where the subwoofer is.

  2. Calibrate the microphone

    Tap Calibrate Microphone. Head unit microphones are not lab instruments: their response at extreme lows and highs differs noticeably from human hearing. Calibration profiles the capsule so microphone colorations are not mistakenly attributed to your car.

    What happens during this ~1-minute pass:

    • level check — a brief multi-speaker burst to align sweep output with your microphone's sensitivity;
    • sweeps — smooth sine sweeps from low to high frequencies through each speaker and subwoofer in turn;
    • volume steps — about 20 s of a quiet reference tone while volume steps automatically from 32 down to 1. This measures the exact decibel drop of each volume step on your head unit.

    Run this once. Repeat only if you reposition the microphone or swap the amplifier.

  3. Measure the cabin and create a preset

    Tap Calibrate Cabin & Create Preset. The wizard prompts for four final choices:

    • Subwoofer Configuration — whether the sub is active;
    • Target Sound Profile: Harman (warm, standard automotive target), Dolby Atmos, Club / Bass Heavy, Vocal / Podcast, or Studio Flat;
    • Soundstage focus — Driver seat, Front center, Cabin center, or No delays;
    • Distance from microphone to ears.

    Set the volume to your usual listening level before measuring: the new preset's loudness compensation anchors to it. The measurement pass picks its own volume automatically. The stages progress as follows:

    • volume selection, about 4 s: silence, then a dual test tone 80 Hz + 1 kHz. The app selects a step between 6 and 18 where bass rises clear above cabin noise without distortion. A noisy cabin does not abort the pass: ambient noise is recorded into the report;
    • noise floor recording;
    • individual speaker sweeps;
    • time-alignment delay calculation;
    • preset synthesis;
    • control sweep — all speakers simultaneously through the synthesized preset. This confirms whether the sub and door mid-bass sum in phase or cancel at the crossover point.

  4. Review findings and apply

    The Calibration Results & Auto-EQ report details your system's acoustics:

    • What the measurement found — detected acoustic faults flagged as Certain, Likely, or Possibly, with actionable remedies;
    • crossover frequency — where door speakers hand low frequencies over to the subwoofer;
    • channel delay settings;
    • synthesized Auto-EQ correction curve.

    Bottom actions:

    • Apply Auto-EQ — saves and activates the synthesized preset;
    • Save Archive (.zip) — saves full diagnostic data to share with us;
    • Close — exit without changing current DSP settings.

    Subwoofer phase suggestions (0/180 switch or wiring swap) only appear if the control sweep physically proved phase cancellation.

  5. Tune loudness compensation

    Open the Loudness tab. Human hearing perceives bass less efficiently at low listening levels. Loudness progressively boosts low frequencies as you lower the volume.

    • Calibration point — volume step above which loudness applies zero boost. For measured presets, it anchors to the normal listening volume at which you launched the measurement. If that was lower than the measurement sweep volume, the app sets an anchor 18 dB louder than your listening level, between steps 16 and 24;
    • Correction intensity — scaling factor applied to the equal-loudness curve.

    The app computes attenuation in actual decibels measured during step 2 rather than arbitrary volume steps. An automated validator at the bottom inspects settings: if any parameter conflicts, tap Set it correctly to fix it instantly.

  6. Capture RTA zero

    Open Audio Check → Pink Noise → RTA zero. This establishes a fixed reference baseline for the real-time analyzer curve instead of a floating average.

    A single tap captures both zero references, synthesized and microphone, regardless of the active display mode. For several seconds, the app temporarily sets the volume at which the microphone was calibrated, then restores your previous volume. The microphone zero locks to that step, so the curve no longer drifts when you adjust volume.

  7. Sync visualizer with audio playback

    wDSP Settings → Sound sync → Synchronise. The app calculates audio pipeline latency relative to screen rendering on your hardware. Spectrum bars will then bounce tightly in sync with the beat.

Volume scale behavior

Hardware volume steps are non-linear. Below is the profile measured on our reference stand. Your values will be measured automatically by the app.

step 32 → 13 ≈ 1 dB per step step 12 → 8 ≈ 2 dB per step step 7 → 5 ≈ 3 dB per step step 4 → 1 ≈ 5–6 dB per step total 32 → 1 ≈ 62 dB

Lowering volume by five steps near the top drops level by roughly 5 dB, whereas five steps near the bottom drops it by nearly 25 dB. This is why loudness compensation relies on calibrated decibels rather than step counts.

Main screen tabs

Equalizer
16 bands, preset selector at the top, and a Subwoofer section: low-pass cutoff frequency and gain. Each slider represents dB change at that band center. Adjacent bands overlap smoothly with proper summing so three consecutive +4 sliders produce a uniform +4 shelf rather than an exaggerated bump. The curve displays the exact response sent to the DSP hardware.
Loudness
Equal-loudness compensation (Loudness), threshold point, and boost depth. Sub compensation extends loudness boost to the subwoofer. Trim Highs softens treble harshness at high volumes. Ultra Bass provides an extra low-end lift below a selectable volume threshold. The preview graph illustrates the exact dynamic boost curve.
Delays
Per-channel time alignment in milliseconds and centimeters so sound waves from all speakers arrive at your ears simultaneously. Calculated automatically during cabin calibration. Move fronts together / Move rears together preserve inter-channel delay offsets while adjusting pairs. Also houses Surround and Rear-Fill processing.
Other
Door speaker bass tuning: high-pass Filter prevents door rattle and offloads bass to the subwoofer; Boost enhances selectable bass frequencies. Separate front and rear controls; Same as front mirrors front settings. Also includes fader and balance controls.
GALA
Speed-dependent volume compensation via GPS to overcome road noise. Speed step sets the speed interval, Activation Threshold sets engagement speed, and Max Compensation defines the gain ceiling. Automatically suspended during phone calls.
Audio Check
Auditory verification signals: song stems (isolated bass, vocals, drums, melody) in any playback combination, calibrated pink noise, sine generator, sweep tone, sequential per-speaker checks, and Only Sub mode. All signals pass through the active preset. Fader and sub-solo settings apply temporarily during testing and do not alter saved presets.

Spectrum and RTA

Switch between spectrum modes via the Spectrum toggle above the equalizer. Supports classic bar graph or live RTA curve, configurable in settings.

Music
Pre-DSP digital audio stream directly from the media player.
Synthesized
Music spectrum combined with active preset response curves: what the preset intends to achieve. Volume-independent: curves remain identical at step 5 and step 32, with only dynamic loudness curves varying.
Microphone
Actual sound captured by the cabin microphone. Scaled to align with the synthesized curve. When tuned, both curves match closely; residual divergence highlights cabin acoustic reflections.

FM radio playback cannot display synthesized spectrum because the analog radio tuner routes directly to hardware without passing through the Android audio pipeline. In radio mode, the visualizer uses microphone input.

Presets and automation

  • Preset manager on the main screen: create, rename, delete, export, and import.
  • Preset Automation binds specific presets to media apps: radio uses one profile, Bluetooth streaming another. A dedicated profile handles Phone Call. Global Default applies whenever no app binding matches.
  • Volume is stored independently per source, matching native head unit behavior.
  • Backup All Settings and Restore Settings: backs up full configuration with presets or settings alone.

Widget and screensaver

Status Bar Visualizer — embedded mini-spectrum in the Android status bar: adjustable width, horizontal offset, height, visualization style (Classic bars, Outrun, VU meter, Oscilloscope), and color palettes.

Screensaver — full-screen visualizer when the unit is idle: configurable timeout, Day/Night brightness profiles, and app exclusion blacklist. Navigation apps are never occluded. A single tap dismisses the screensaver; tapping album art returns directly to the active media player.

UI Style: Classic (Original) — original author interface layout; Modern — refreshed responsive design. Both styles support native Day and Night themes.

Troubleshooting

"Microphone temporarily busy, using calculated spectrum"

Another app holds exclusive microphone access, most commonly Google Assistant. Reboot the head unit: wDSP initializes early to claim the microphone first. On rooted units, wDSP acquires the handle automatically.

Measurement reports "measured in noise"

The cabin was not quiet enough. The app selects sweep volume automatically, so reduce cabin noise: shut all doors and windows, turn off the engine and climate fan, and measure again.

Volume steps failed to record

Transient noise interrupted the tone sequence, or the microphone failed to register the level check. The app retains previously calibrated step tables; without a table, it falls back to nominal steps. Inspect volume_steps.txt inside the exported archive, and rerun microphone calibration.

Microphone RTA baseline drifts when adjusting volume

Microphone RTA zero was calibrated before volume-tracking was enabled. Re-zero RTA on pink noise (step 6).

Red "Microphone overloaded" warning above the equalizer

The microphone signal is so loud it clips against the ceiling, most often on heavy bass. While overloaded, the microphone curve is displayed with AGC: its level is matched to the synthesized spectrum and the curve shape is approximate. Once the level drops, the warning disappears and the curve returns to its calibrated zero. If the warning appears even at normal listening volume, lower microphone sensitivity in head unit settings.

Audio tone changes after reboot

DSP parameters are restored at boot by the background service without launching the UI. Ensure Background work is granted and wDSP is allowed to stay alive during sleep.

Sharing measurements

After completing cabin calibration, tap Save Archive (.zip). The archive packages measurement logs, microphone response data, volume step calibrations, and a synchronized system bugreport.

Share your archive in the Telegram community t.me/wDSPapp, in the calibration topic t.me/wDSPapp/79, or via direct forum message. Avoid sending bare uncompressed files as individual attachments are easily fragmented.

Include your car's make, model, microphone position, and amplifier configuration when posting.
